# R-116 — market of path 3 (narrowband satellite messaging) and the effect of direct-to-cell — 2026-11-15

> Asked by the owner on 2026-11-15. Desk research only: eight web searches with generic queries (no project or
> owner identifiers), read through search summaries; no page fetched in full, no integrator contacted, no real
> traffic corpus obtained. Those two parts of R-116 (corpus, conversation with an integrator) remain undone and
> cannot be done from a terminal.
> Labels: REPORTED (third party, source named, not verified here), MEASURED (own, document named), DERIVED
> (arithmetic on the above), INFERENCE.

## 0. Result first — weak points before anything else

1. **Desk verdict: R-116 is NOT validated, and what was found points against path 3.** The segment where a byte is
   expensive enough is small per device, mostly machine telemetry (where Redukt has no advantage, E3 / E3b), and
   the human free-text part of it — the only traffic where Mamba-130M wins clearly — is the part that
   direct-to-cell is moving to flat-rate phones.
2. **The average Iridium IoT subscriber pays the network $7.63 per month** (IoT data ARPU, Q3 2026, down 4 % in a
   year; 2,163,000 IoT data subscribers; commercial IoT data revenue $46.0M in Q1 2026) (REPORTED, Iridium
   quarterly releases). The whole Iridium IoT airtime pool is therefore about $190-200M per year at wholesale
   (DERIVED), and a compressor can only save a fraction of the fraction of it that is compressible text.
3. **At that ARPU a typical device moves kilobytes per month.** At the SBD reseller prices of the path-3 document
   ($0.79-3.15 per KB), $7.63 buys 2-10 KB a month (DERIVED; retail prices against wholesale ARPU, so the true
   volume is lower still). That is tracker and sensor traffic, hand-packed in binary (17 bytes for a full position
   report, REPORTED), not text.
4. **Direct-to-cell does not make the narrowband byte cheap everywhere; it removes the human texter.** Consumer
   satellite texting is now flat-rate or free: T-Satellite about $10 per month (included in top plans), Apple's
   satellite messaging free with the device (REPORTED, secondary sources that disagree on details). A flat-rate
   message has no per-byte price, so compression has no value to the sender. Standards-based NB-IoT NTN is priced
   around $0.01-0.05 per KB (REPORTED, a vendor blog; other quotes $0.30 and $0.90 per KB) — 16-300x below SBD
   but still $10,000-50,000 per GB, far above the compute cost. So the byte stays expensive for machines and stops
   being priced for people.
5. **The pincer (INFERENCE from 1-4 and E3 / E3b).** Human free text, where Mamba-130M is 25-48 % smaller than any
   primed public compressor, is leaving the byte-priced links. Machine telemetry, which stays, is packed by hand
   or, when it is textual and repeats, is compressed as well or better by a 9 MB public coder with a sample
   (primed Mamba is 13-17 % ahead of lpaq1 and 11-19 % behind paq8px). And a tracker-class modem cannot run a
   130 MB model.
6. **A paying niche exists and is occupied: e-mail and weather over satellite phones for offshore sailing.**
   XGate, OCENSMail / OneMail, PredictMail sell compression bundled with airtime ("up to 95 %", "up to 20x"
   — marketing figures, not benchmarks) (REPORTED). It proves willingness to pay; it is also small, served by
   incumbents who own the customer and the airtime contract, and it is the traffic Starlink maritime is taking
   ($1-6 per GB, path-3 document).
7. Limits. (a) Search summaries of secondary sources; several conflict (T-Satellite price $10 / $15 / $20;
   inReach overage $0.50 / $0.95 / $1.00 per message). (b) Two ownership reports — Globalstar bought by Amazon,
   Iridium to be acquired by Rocket Lab — come from secondary sites only and are recorded as REPORTED, unverified.
   (c) No NTN price list from an operator was found; the per-KB band is a vendor's estimate. (d) No figure exists
   here for the share of text-like traffic on any network: the upper bound of section 3 is illustrative.
   (e) Government and defence use (Enhanced SBD, 4,095-byte messages) was not researched and may behave
   differently. (f) Nothing was measured for this document.

DERIVED: 2.16M subscribers x $7.63 x 12 = about $198M per year of Iridium IoT data revenue. Growth comes from
subscribers (+9 %), not from spend per device (ARPU falling): more small devices, not more bytes per device.

DERIVED: consumer messengers are billed per message, not per byte. A 160-character message already fits in one
SBD burst; shrinking it saves the end user nothing. Compression would save the device vendor airtime at wholesale
— one buyer per device brand, with its own firmware and its own incentives.

## 2. Direct-to-cell and standards-based NTN (REPORTED unless marked)

| Service | What it does today | Price | Effect on path 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starlink direct to cell (T-Satellite and partner carriers) | texts, location, emergency alerts; thin app data in some markets; voice not live | about $10 per month, included in top plans; other sources say $15-20 | human texting off-grid becomes flat-rate on the phone people already own |
| Apple satellite messaging (Globalstar) | texts and SOS from the phone | free with the device for a period | same; "are satellite messengers dead?" is a live question in the outdoor press |
| NB-IoT NTN (Skylo, Sateliot, Viasat, Iridium NTN Direct) | small, infrequent machine messages on standard chipsets | about $0.01-0.05 per KB with per-message minimums; older quotes $0.30 and $0.90 per KB; modules $8-15, $1-3 per device and month | cheaper than SBD below 30-50 KB per month per device; above that proprietary services (IMT) are cheaper; byte still priced, traffic is machine telemetry |
| Iridium's answer | NTN Direct aimed first at low-cost IoT; management positions the network for industrial and government use, not mass-market phones | not published | the incumbent itself is moving toward cheaper, smaller messages |

Analyst view (secondary): new LEO entrants "threaten to commoditize messaging-level connectivity, where Iridium
has historically earned premium prices"; Iridium's maritime and aviation broadband revenue fell 9 % in Q4 2025.

Reading (INFERENCE):
- The price per byte on narrowband machine links falls by one to two orders of magnitude where NTN applies, and
  stays three to four orders above the compute cost. Path 3's arithmetic ("the byte is worth more than the
  compute") survives for machines.
- What does not survive is the traffic mix. The human who typed free text into a satellite messenger is the user
  direct-to-cell serves at a flat rate. The remaining byte-priced traffic is the kind E3 / E3b showed needs a
  sample, not a pretrained model.

## 3. How large could the prize be (DERIVED, illustrative — the text share is unknown)

Let f be the share of Iridium IoT airtime revenue that is compressible free text sent by terminals able to run a
130 MB model (a Linux-class gateway, not a tracker modem). Savings on that share against the best free alternative:
25-48 % on natural language (MEASURED, E3).

| f | Airtime on text, $M per year | Customer saving at 25-48 %, $M per year | Vendor revenue at a 10-30 % share of the saving and 100 % adoption |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 % | 2.0 | 0.5-1.0 | $0.05-0.3M |
| 5 % | 9.9 | 2.5-4.8 | $0.25-1.4M |
| 10 % | 19.8 | 5.0-9.5 | $0.5-2.9M |

Nothing found supports f above a few percent: the ARPU says kilobytes per month, the documentation says binary
trackers, and the consumer messengers bill per message. Other networks (Inmarsat / Viasat narrowband, Globalstar,
ORBCOMM) add volume of the same kind; they do not change the order of magnitude. At 100 % adoption and the
generous row, the whole market is a few million dollars a year, shared with whoever owns the terminal.

## 4. Verdict and what it means for the roadmap

| R-116 item | Status after this document |
|---|---|
| Market size (terminals, MB per month, willingness to pay) | desk estimate done: small per device, falling ARPU, telemetry-dominated; willingness to pay proven only in sailing e-mail, an occupied niche |
| Effect of direct-to-cell | done: removes the flat-rate human texter; lowers machine byte prices 16-300x where NTN applies, still far above compute |
| Real corpus of the segment | NOT done |
| Conversation with a satellite communications integrator | NOT done |

**Recommendation (the decision is the owner's).** Do not write code for path 3 (R-114 message framing, R-115
ARM64). The single cheap step that could still overturn this is one conversation with an integrator or a sailing
e-mail vendor, asking one question: what share of your customers' byte-priced traffic is free text, and would you
bundle a 130 MB compressor to shrink it by a quarter to a half? If the answer is "little" or "no", path 3 closes
and no segment remains where Redukt's measured advantage meets a price.

**What is left of the project's case after E2-E4 and this document (INFERENCE).** A measured, real advantage on
natural-language text (prose 43-58 % smaller than paq8px; short messages 25-48 %), at a ratio level with the best
published neural compressors, with no buyer identified: storage values text at about $0.22 per raw GB over seven
years, and the links where a byte is expensive do not carry much text. The honest options are the owner's to weigh:
keep Redukt as a demonstrated research / portfolio artefact; look for a text-heavy buyer outside storage and
satellite (none identified here); or stop.
